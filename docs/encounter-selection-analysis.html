Eligibility sensitivity

The reconstructed-state analysis used 94 ordinary encounters and excluded 0 that the current data model considered ineligible. The condition-free sensitivity analysis used 94 and excluded 0.

A positive NPC-minus-offer log-score difference favors selecting an NPC uniformly before selecting one of its offers. Reconstructed difference: -0.2439 bits/encounter; condition-free difference: -0.2191 bits/encounter.

Data-quality notes

Offer identity, order, case index, and run boundaries are treated as authoritative. Resources, counters, flags, and eligibility are sensitivity evidence because the game and simulator model may have drifted. Game version and capture context are unknown.

No parser or sequence-integrity warnings were recorded.

Method

Manual adjustments and known cadence, forced, or override encounters are retained in totals but excluded from ordinary-selector tests. Order-only tests shuffle observed NPC/offer pairs within each run segment, preserving marginal counts and preventing transitions across discontinuities. Frequency tests simulate independent choices from each case’s candidate set.

Primary statistics cover NPC and offer goodness of fit, immediate repetition, longest streak, five-draw diversity, lagged mutual information, within-NPC offer repetition, cadence-slot association, run heterogeneity, and a categorical change-point scan. Recorded analyses use 100,000 deterministic resamples with seed 0. Monte Carlo p-values use (extreme + 1) / (resamples + 1).

Research basis: NIST on sparse categorical goodness of fit; Phipson and Smyth on valid permutation p-values; Papapetrou and Kugiumtzis on mutual-information randomization for categorical sequences; and NIST on randomness diagnostics.

Evidence labels

  • Not detected: Holm-adjusted p > 0.05.
  • Tentative: adjusted p ≤ 0.05 without at least three independently consistent runs.
  • Repeated: at least three runs, adjusted p ≤ 0.05, and consistent leave-one-run-out direction.
  • Model-sensitive: reconstructed and condition-free eligibility analyses disagree.
