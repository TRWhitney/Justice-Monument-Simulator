Suggested Rules Simulation Analysis

Last run: 2026-08-07

Outcome: add a guarded soft preference for Fizarre Drink: Chest Magnet and narrow Mister Bribe: Approval Lock's Harbinger safety window from three cases to two. A later ablation audit removed three inherited Vercel-era hard constraints that degraded configured-horizon utility and survival: Timmy Stinky/Poopy approval, Scripticus Promise Me before Harbinger, and Honcho Life Insurance above 1 Mental Health.

Method

The experiment used the built-in Justice dataset, uniform eligible-offer selection, the balanced utility preset, and the application's standard starting state: case 1, 5 Court Coins, 3 Popularity, 1 Mental Health, no dismissals, and no Retirement Chests. Each old/new comparison used the same integer seeds, beginning at zero. Reported confidence intervals are normal-approximation 95% intervals over paired per-seed differences.

Runs exercised the planner's future-policy simulation directly. The 12-case experiment matches the configured lookahead horizon; the 60-case experiment checks whether the effect persists beyond that horizon. This is model evidence, not a claim about the game's unknown real encounter distribution.

Accepted changes

  • Chest Magnet: a +1 approval bias applies only when coins >= case_scale + harbinger_cost. The small bias recognizes the value of the next three Retirement Chester encounters, while the reserve condition and soft preference let rollout evidence reject unsafe purchases. Hard-requiring approval and a +5 bias were tested but rejected as unnecessarily aggressive.
  • Approval Lock: approving is forbidden only when harbinger_in is one or two. Acceptance is already the first of the three lock cases, so a lock taken three cases before Harbinger expires in time. With seed 123 and the default planner, case 2 changed from reject to approve while case 3 remained reject.

Confirmation and state sensitivity

Five candidates advanced to 2,500 paired 60-case runs. Crypto Drip, Brain for Sale, Vibe Check, and Reaper removal all had confidence intervals spanning zero for both utility and chest yield. Dynamic Vitality produced +0.0116 chests (95% CI +0.0029 to +0.0203), but its utility interval crossed zero and mean ending case fell by 0.0668.

Vitality also improved the default 12-case result by +0.0072 chests and +0.1275 utility, with both intervals above zero. It failed the required state-sensitivity check: from a healthy early-run state, allowing the trade at 3+, 4+, or 5+ Mental Health raised deaths by 3.58, 1.83, and 1.25 percentage points respectively. The most conservative 5+ version reduced safe-preset utility by 2.1024 (95% CI -4.1065 to -0.0982) and raised deaths by 1.38 percentage points. Because suggested rules are shared across starting states and risk presets, the existing Vitality restriction remains.

Follow-up decision: no additional suggested rules were accepted. The current dataset remains the Pareto-safe choice among the trialed variants; apparent default-state gains either vanished under confirmation or produced a measurable safety regression elsewhere.

Exploratory discovery pass

A third pass deliberately searched beyond the earlier proposals. It screened a +3 approval preference and a +3 rejection preference for every one of the 73 ordinary offers (146 variants), including the 48 ordinary offers with no existing suggested rule. It separately tested dismissal preferences, resource-policy rules spanning multiple offers, all 12 Harbinger and Gratefulbinger offers, and portfolios of individually weak rules. Screening used paired seeds and promoted apparent gains to larger independent comparisons; no screening result was accepted on its own.

Broad action search

The 250-seed, 40-case sweep surfaced Coin Flip rejection, Crypto Drip approval, Bored Loan approval, Ghost Scare rejection, IOU rejection, No School approval, Student rejection, Lost Coins rejection, Dirty Handshake rejection, Magic Rupie rejection, and Pop Swap approval as the largest unfamiliar signals. Ten leading variants were rerun over 1,000 paired 60-case seeds. Nine had utility intervals spanning zero or moved in the opposite direction. Pop Swap was the largest remaining estimate at +0.4898 utility, but its wide 95% interval (-0.3908 to +1.3704) did not establish an improvement.

No School was the closest state-independent idea. Approval biases from +1 through +5 behaved nearly identically over 1,200 paired 60-case runs; the +1 result was +0.401 utility with a 95% interval of -0.016 to +0.818. At the configured 12-case horizon and 4,000 paired seeds, that same rule instead reduced utility by 0.1370 (95% CI -0.2663 to -0.0077) and raised death probability by 0.10 percentage points (95% CI +0.002 to +0.198 percentage points). Coin-reserve and Timmy-counter guards were also harmful or behaviorally redundant, so the family was rejected.

Special encounters and rule interactions

Approval, rejection, and dismissal preferences were screened across all Harbinger and Gratefulbinger offers. Rent Due approval was the only material lead: requiring payment when coins covered three times the current Harbinger cost produced +0.3968 utility over 1,800 paired 60-case runs, but its 95% interval (-0.0189 to +0.8124) crossed zero. At 3,000 paired 12-case seeds it was slightly negative (-0.0722 utility; 95% CI -0.1740 to +0.0296). Mental Health, dismissal, and soft-preference guards made it neutral or worse. Shark Attack and Busted Bills variants also failed confirmation.

Five weak candidates were then combined into long-term, safety, Crypto-plus-Rent, No-School-plus-Rent, and all-five portfolios. Only No-School-plus-Rent cleared zero for long-run utility: +0.4808 (95% CI +0.0256 to +0.9360) over 1,500 paired 60-case seeds, while its chest, death, and ending-case intervals all included zero. The required 4,000-seed, 12-case check reversed the result to -0.2062 utility (95% CI -0.3573 to -0.0551), again without an established chest or survival benefit.

Exploratory decision: accept no novel rule. The broad search found useful hypotheses, but every new candidate either disappeared under paired confirmation, conflicted with the configured horizon, was redundant with the planner, or introduced a resource or safety tradeoff without a reliable primary outcome gain.

Inherited-rule ablation audit

All 18 rules present after the earlier refinement were tested by removing exactly one rule from the current policy and replaying identical seeds. The initial screen used 700 paired 60-case runs; every rule was then tested over 3,000 paired runs at the configured 12-case horizon. Positive values below mean that removing the inherited rule improved balanced utility. Apparent degraders were confirmed over 5,000 paired seeds under balanced, safe, and greedy scoring. Dormant means the ablation did not change a terminal outcome in the configured-horizon screen; it does not prove that the rule can never activate.

Timmy's result came entirely from Stinky Heads, where forced approval loses one case-scaled Popularity while rejection gains one. The Poopy Head constraint was behaviorally redundant, so the combined inherited rule was removed rather than retaining unsupported guidance. Promise Me awards case-scaled coins and forces the next approval; immediately before Harbinger that obligation usually aligns with paying Harbinger, making the blanket prohibition counterproductive. The Life Insurance constraint prevented the planner from selecting a temporary four-health buffer at any health above one even when rollout evidence favored it. Removing the hard constraint leaves the choice to simulated action scoring, while the delayed repayment trigger remains encoded in the resulting state.

Removing all three together was confirmed over 8,000 paired 12-case seeds. Balanced utility increased by 1.4524 (95% CI +1.1427 to +1.7622), expected chests increased by 0.0069 (+0.0036 to +0.0102), death probability fell by 0.84 percentage points (-1.06 to -0.62), and mean ending case increased by 0.0286 (+0.0208 to +0.0364). Safe and greedy utility also improved. At 60 cases, the combined removal retained a +1.0372 utility improvement (95% CI +0.3235 to +1.7510) and a 0.33 percentage-point death reduction (95% CI -0.61 to -0.06).

Ablation decision: remove the three degrading inherited constraints and retain the other 15 rules. Chest Heist and Vitality were not removed merely to gain chests because their ablations did not establish a survival improvement; prior state-sensitivity evidence also found a Vitality safety regression.

Simulation reliability correction

Before accepting the data result, two rollout edge cases were corrected: an encounter-triggered death is now returned as the terminal state, and constraints fall back when they leave no feasible action. Focused unit tests cover both cases. Candidate comparisons above were rerun only after these corrections, preventing dead-end policies from appearing safer than they are.

Limits and security

Results depend on the simulator's uniform encounter assumption and the uncertain probabilities documented in the data open-questions report. They should be rerun if observed encounter weights or unresolved outcome probabilities change. The new condition uses the existing whitelisted expression evaluator; it adds no dynamic evaluation or external input path.
